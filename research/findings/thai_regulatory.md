## 1. Thai cosmetic claims / advertising rules

### 1.1 Cosmetics Act B.E. 2558 (2015) - core provisions [LAW]

Source for this subsection unless stated otherwise: [Cosmetics Act B.E. 2558, Thai FDA English translation](https://catalog.fda.moph.go.th/dataset/1e51956a-5718-4aa9-8b23-076349ed1856/resource/848bf9fe-ee17-4d7d-89e3-f4d6e304e6eb/download/cosmetics-act-b.e.-2015.pdf).

- **Definition of "cosmetic" (s.4)**: a substance intended to be applied (rubbed, massaged, sprinkled, sprayed, dropped, etc.) to the **external parts of the human body**, teeth and oral mucosa, **exclusively or mainly to clean, perfume, change appearance, correct body odour, protect or keep them in good condition**. Thai text: "...โดยมีวัตถุประสงค์เพื่อความสะอาด ความสวยงาม หรือเปลี่ยนแปลงลักษณะที่ปรากฏ หรือระงับกลิ่นกาย หรือปกป้องดูแลส่วนต่างๆ นั้น ให้อยู่ในสภาพดี" ([FDA manual 2559, p.5](https://www.premacare.co.th/assets/file/law/202103/171258_%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B9%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%82%E0%B8%86%E0%B8%A9%E0%B8%93%E0%B8%B2%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B7%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87.pdf)). Everything the AI says about what a product "does" must fit inside these purposes.
- **"Statement" (ข้อความ)** includes text, **pictures**, film, light, sound, symbols or any act conveying meaning. **"Advertising" (โฆษณา)** = any act, by any means, making the public see, hear or know a statement **for commercial benefit**. **"Advertising media"** expressly includes telephone and **electronic media** (s.4).
- **Notification (จดแจ้ง), s.14**: anyone manufacturing, importing for sale, or contracting manufacture of cosmetics must notify and receive a notification receipt **before** manufacturing/importing. Receipt valid **3 years** (s.15). Penalty for breach of s.14 para 1: imprisonment up to 6 months and/or fine up to THB 50,000 (s.62). The 2022 amendment (Cosmetics Act (No. 2) B.E. 2565) mainly changed administrative review procedures and fees, not the advertising sections ([ChemLinked](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/cosmetic-news/thailand-issues-amendments-to-the-cosmetic-act-be-2558-2015)).
- **Advertising, s.41**: advertising must not use statements that are unfair to consumers or harmful to society, whether about origin, condition, quality, quantity or properties. Deemed unfair:
  1. **false or exaggerated** statements (ข้อความที่เป็นเท็จหรือเกินความจริง);
  2. statements causing **misunderstanding of the essential nature** of the cosmetic, whether or not by citing reports/statistics that are untrue or exaggerated;
  3. statements showing **properties of treating disease, or a purpose that is not a cosmetic purpose** (ข้อความที่แสดงสรรพคุณที่เป็นการรักษาโรคหรือที่มิใช่จุดมุ่งหมายเป็นเครื่องสำอาง);
  4. statements implying aphrodisiac / sexual-enhancement properties;
  5. statements supporting illegal/immoral acts or degrading national culture;
  6. statements causing disunity among the public;
  7. other statements prescribed by Ministerial Regulation.
  Obvious puffery that the public knows cannot be true is not caught by (1).
- **s.42**: advertising must not be done by a method harmful to health/mind/morals or that **annoys consumers** (อาจก่อให้เกิดความเดือดร้อนรำคาญแก่ผู้บริโภค) - relevant to push/broadcast frequency.
- **Enforcement powers**: the **Secretary-General of the Thai FDA** (with the Cosmetic Committee) may order warnings, restrict or prohibit advertising (s.43); order amendment, prohibit statements, prohibit the advertisement/method, or order **corrective advertising** (s.44); and **demand proof** of any claim - if the advertiser cannot prove it, s.44 orders follow (s.45). Orders can be addressed to the **notifier or the advertiser**.
- **Advance opinion, s.46**: a notifier/advertiser unsure about an advertisement may ask the Committee for an opinion; answer due within **60 days** (silence = approval); acting on the opinion is **not a criminal offence**. [INTERPRETATION] This is a practical route to de-risk the AI's standard claim library.
- **No pre-approval licence** is required for cosmetic advertising; the regime is post-market surveillance ([Thai FDA news, 11 Sep 2025](https://www.fda.moph.go.th/news/110968); [English version](https://en.fda.moph.go.th/news/thai-fda-reminds-no-advertising-license-required-for-cosmetics-but-truthful-and-lawful-claims-are)).
- **Penalties**: advertising in breach of s.41 or s.42: **imprisonment up to 1 year and/or fine up to THB 100,000 (s.84)**; disobeying s.43/s.44 orders: same (s.85); **continuing offence: daily fine up to THB 10,000 (s.88)**. Thai text: "มาตรา ๘๔ ผู้ใดโฆษณาโดยไม่เป็นไปตามมาตรา ๔๑ หรือไม่ปฏิบัติตามมาตรา ๔๒ ต้องระวางโทษจำคุกไม่เกินหนึ่งปี หรือปรับไม่เกินหนึ่งแสนบาท หรือทั้งจำทั้งปรับ" ([FDA manual 2559, p.7](https://www.premacare.co.th/assets/file/law/202103/171258_%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B9%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%82%E0%B8%86%E0%B8%A9%E0%B8%93%E0%B8%B2%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B7%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87.pdf)). Offences punishable by fine only or up to 1 year can be **settled by FDA compounding** (s.90); the B.E. 2559 compounding schedule printed in the 2559 manual shows THB 30,000 / 60,000 / 100,000 for 1st/2nd/3rd advertising offences and referral for prosecution thereafter (schedule may have been updated - verify).
- **Who is liable**: s.84 applies to "any person who advertises" (ผู้ใดโฆษณา) - not only the notifier. The FDA's 2567 manual is explicitly addressed to manufacturers, sellers and influencers ([Thai FDA, 9 Oct 2024](https://www.fda.moph.go.th/news/09102567)).

**Ministerial Regulation under s.41(7) (2024)** [LAW - publication detail to verify]: Cabinet approved on 19 Nov 2024 a Ministerial Regulation adding two further prohibited statement types: (1) statements making people understand the cosmetic is **used inside the human body** (except sanitary/tampon-type and oral products); (2) statements making people understand it is **injected, used with devices that invade the body, or used to push substances deeper than the epidermis (ลึกกว่าชั้นหนังกำพร้า)**, e.g. mesotherapy-style claims ([MGR Online 19 Nov 2024](https://mgronline.com/politics/detail/9670000111257); [Thansettakij](https://www.thansettakij.com/health/605737); [ChemLinked 22 Nov 2024](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/cosmetic-news/thailand-expands-rules-on-unacceptable-cosmetic-advertising-claims)). We did not retrieve the Royal Gazette text; the exact wording and effective date must be confirmed.

### 1.2 Is a 1:1 AI chat reply "advertising"? [INTERPRETATION]

The statutory definition is broad ("any act by any means" to make "the public" see/hear/know a statement "for commercial benefit", via media including electronic media). A brand-operated chatbot that systematically delivers product claims to every LINE follower is, conservatively, **advertising**, even though each reply is one-to-one. The same words are also sales-talk by the seller, caught in parallel by the Consumer Protection Act (section 3.1) and, if false, potentially the Computer Crime Act (3.5). **Recommended position: treat every product-related sentence the AI produces - including image captions, before/after pictures, emojis/symbols - as an advertisement subject to s.41.**

### 1.3 Thai FDA Cosmetic Advertising Manual (คู่มือการโฆษณาเครื่องสำอาง) [GUIDELINE]

- **Latest version**: "ฉบับปรับปรุง พ.ศ. 2567", dated/updated 25 Sep 2024 and publicised by the Thai FDA on 9-11 Oct 2024 ([Thai FDA](https://www.fda.moph.go.th/news/09102567); [ChemLinked](https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/news/cosmetic-news/thailand-updates-advertising-guideline-for-cosmetics)). Re-affirmed as the reference document in Sept 2025 ([Thai FDA](https://www.fda.moph.go.th/news/110968)). Structure: general principles; prohibited advertising types with examples; do/don't wording for **~22-23 product groups** (whitening, anti-wrinkle, anti-acne, contour/firming, exfoliation, sunscreen, rinse-off cleansers, anti-dandruff, hair/scalp, perm/colour, deodorant, fragrance, nail, oral care, teeth bleaching, anti-blue-light, skin balancing/microbiome, natural claims, detox, certification symbols, sales comparisons, statistical/test claims, and cannabis/hemp per ChemLinked); penalties.
- **Status**: the manual is guidance compiling the Act, ministerial regulations, notifications, ASEAN Cosmetic Claims Guideline and committee resolutions; officials use it as the yardstick when assessing advertisements (preface of [2559 edition](https://www.premacare.co.th/assets/file/law/202103/171258_%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B9%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%82%E0%B8%86%E0%B8%A9%E0%B8%93%E0%B8%B2%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B7%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87.pdf)). A later FDA note on inappropriate texts states such guidance "does not impose new requirements" and assessment is case-by-case in context ([ChemRadar/GlobalCosing, Aug 2026](https://globalcosing.chemradar.com/en/news/detail/fwiwd97ilp1c)).

**Named s.41 violation patterns (2567, via TNP/TGC; most also in 2559 p.12):**

- s.41(1) hard-to-prove absolutes: "ที่สุด", "ที่สุดในโลก", "ไร้สารพิษ", "ปลอดภัย ไร้สารตกค้าง ปราศจากสารอันตราย", "ไม่ระคายเคืองผิว" (listed by TGC), "ไร้ผลข้างเคียง", "จบทุกปัญหาผิว / จบทุกปัญหาสิว".
- s.41(1) implying **Thai FDA / Dept. of Medical Sciences certifies quality or safety**: "ผ่านมาตรฐานจากองค์การอาหารและยา", "ผ่านการรับรองจาก อย.", "อย. รับรองความปลอดภัย".
- s.41(1) **comparison with medical procedures**: "แค่ทาก็เท่ากับฉีด", "ขาวไวกว่าฉีด", "หน้าเรียวไม่ง้อโบท็อกซ์", "ผิวเต่งตึงเสมือนยิงเลเซอร์".
- s.41(2)/(3) **health effect**: "ฟื้นฟูผิวหนังให้แข็งแรง", "ดูดซับของเสียตกค้างใต้ผิว", "ล้างสารพิษ", "กระตุ้นการไหลเวียนของเลือด".
- **body structure**: "เสริม/ลดหน้าอก", "สลายไขมัน", "ยกกระชับ ปรับรูปหน้าเรียว", "กระชับสัดส่วน", "ลดความอ้วน", "ทำให้สูงขึ้น".
- **body function**: "กระตุ้นการสร้างคอลลาเจนและอีลาสติน", "ลดการสร้างเม็ดสีผิว", "ยับยั้งเอนไซม์ tyrosinase", "ต่อต้านอนุมูลอิสระ", "ลดผมหงอก / เร่งการงอกของเส้นผม หนวด เครา", "ลดผมร่วง", "ลดผมบาง", "บำรุงรากผม".
- s.41(3) **disease treatment**: "รักษาสิว / ป้องกันหรือลดอาการอักเสบของสิว", "ป้องกันหรือรักษาฝ้า", "แก้ผดผื่นคัน", "ระงับหรือยับยั้งเชื้อแบคทีเรีย เชื้อราที่ผิวหนัง", "ลดการอักเสบของผิวหนัง", "ปกป้องผิวจากโรคผิวหนัง".

| 33 | สครับช่วยกระชับรูขุมขน / ลอกผิวใส / แค่ขัดก็ขาว | scrub tightens pores; peels to clear skin | "ช่วยให้ผิวนุ่ม ผิวดูเรียบเนียน"; AHA >= 2.5% without sunscreen: advise avoiding sun / using sunscreen | skin feels soft and looks smooth | G-59 group 5; G-67 (TNP, TGC) |

### 1.5 Cosmetic vs drug borderline [LAW + GUIDELINE]

- **Drug Act B.E. 2510, s.4**: "drugs" include (2) substances intended for the **diagnosis, treatment, relief, cure or prevention of disease**, and (4) substances intended to **affect the health, structure or function of the human body**; limbs (1), (2), (4) exclude things intended for use as **cosmetics** ([Drug Act English translation, Thai FDA](https://en.fda.moph.go.th/media.php?id=504124621688283136&name=2.+Drug+Acts+B.E.2510+and+amendment+2001.pdf); [Thai FDA "What is a drug"](https://en.fda.moph.go.th/entrepreneurs-medicines/what-is-drug-01)).
- The FDA manual uses exactly this line: cosmetic claims must have "no intended effect on **health, structure or any function** of the human body, which are drug properties" (ไม่มีผลมุ่งหมายสำหรับให้เกิดผลแก่สุขภาพ โครงสร้างหรือการกระทำหน้าที่ใดๆ ของร่างกายของมนุษย์ ซึ่งเป็นสรรพคุณทางยา) ([Manual 2559 p.8](https://www.premacare.co.th/assets/file/law/202103/171258_%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B9%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%82%E0%B8%86%E0%B8%A9%E0%B8%93%E0%B8%B2%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B7%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%87.pdf)).
- **Practical test for the AI** [INTERPRETATION]: a sentence crosses into drug territory if it (a) names a disease/condition as something the product treats, prevents or relieves (สิว as disease outcome, ฝ้า, ผื่น, อักเสบ, ติดเชื้อ, แผล); (b) claims a physiological mechanism (collagen synthesis, melanin/tyrosinase inhibition, cell renewal, blood circulation, hormone, sebum-gland regulation); (c) claims structural change (lifting, fat loss, pore size change, hair growth); or (d) implies delivery below the epidermis. The consequence is twofold: an s.41(3) advertising offence, and the risk that the product is treated as an unregistered drug with drug-advertising offences ([Juslaws & Consult, Thai FDA compliance guide, 30 Apr 2026 - lists borderline cosmetic-drug mis-filing and claims exceeding the licence among common enforcement triggers](https://www.juslaws.com/articles/thai-fda-compliance-guide-importers-manufacturers)) [COMMENTARY].

### 1.6 Rules relevant to an AI (or salesperson) giving individual advice

- **No Thai rule specific to AI beauty advisers was found.** The constraints come from general law:
- **Medical Profession Act B.E. 2525** [LAW]: "วิชาชีพเวชกรรม" = acts on humans concerning **examination of disease, diagnosis, treatment, prevention**...; "โรค" includes illness, injury, **abnormality of body or mind and symptoms thereof** (s.4). **s.26** prohibits anyone who is not a licensed practitioner from practising **or showing by any means that they are ready to practise** medicine; **s.43**: imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to THB 30,000 ([Medical Council, Act text](https://www.tmc.or.th/download/law-medical_2525.pdf)). [INTERPRETATION] A bot that tells a customer "you have [skin disease]" and what to use to treat it risks being characterised as diagnosis/treatment; a bot styled as "หมอ/แพทย์ผิวหนัง" risks the "holding out" limb. Hard rules: describe **visible characteristics** only (oiliness, dryness, visible redness, uneven tone, visible pores, fine lines), never name a disease, always include the non-diagnosis disclaimer, and refer to a dermatologist when the image suggests anything beyond cosmetic concern (inflamed/painful lesions, spreading rash, changing moles, wounds, infection signs).
- **FDA manual on medical endorsement** [GUIDELINE]: avoid medical personnel/professional bodies in cosmetic advertising; never imply a hospital/clinic (words "โรงพยาบาล", "คลินิก", medical symbols) - see 1.3.
- **Personalised output is still advertising** - see 1.2; the individual-results qualifier ("ผลลัพธ์ขึ้นอยู่กับสภาพผิวของแต่ละบุคคล") is the FDA's own standard safeguard and should be the AI's default closing line for any efficacy-type statement.
- **Sunscreen and AHA advice**: when recommending sunscreen, include the FDA's correct-use message (amount, 15-30 minutes before exposure, re-apply every 1-2 hours); when recommending AHA-type exfoliants, advise sun avoidance/sunscreen (Manual 2559 groups 5-6).

## 2. PDPA (Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562) for face photos

Statutory references in this section are to the [PDPA English translation hosted by MDES](https://www.mdes.go.th/uploads/tinymce/source/%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%AA/Personal%20Data%20Protection%20Act%202019.pdf) unless stated.

### 2.1 Is a face photo "sensitive personal data" under s.26?

- **[LAW]** s.26 prohibits collecting data on race, ethnic origin, political opinions, beliefs, sexual behaviour, criminal records, **health data**, disability, trade-union information, genetic data, **biometric data** (or other data prescribed by the Committee) **without explicit consent**, subject to narrow exceptions. It defines biometric data as personal data **arising from the use of techniques or technology relating to physical or behavioural characteristics of a person, which can be used to identify that person**, e.g. facial recognition data, iris recognition data, fingerprint recognition data (Thai: ข้อมูลภาพจำลองใบหน้า ข้อมูลจำลองม่านตา ข้อมูลจำลองลายนิ้วมือ).
- **[COMMENTARY]** Thai commentators read this as covering *templates produced by recognition technology*, not ordinary photographs: "ภาพถ่ายโดยทั่วไป ... ไม่ใช่ข้อมูลตามมาตรา 26" ([Bangkokbiznews column by DPOaaS / KMUTT authors](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/tech/1134420)). This is commentary, not a PDPC ruling. No PDPC decision or guideline on skin-analysis photos was found.
- **Why the conservative answer is still "treat as sensitive"** [INTERPRETATION, supported by the sources cited]:
  1. **The pipeline may create biometric data.** The s.26 definition turns on *technical processing of physical features that can identify the person*. If the vision model or any vendor component computes face embeddings/landmark templates capable of identification, or photos are stored and matched to a returning user, the data moves into the definition regardless of Srichand's purpose.
  2. **Inferences may be health data.** "Health data" is listed in s.26 but not defined. Outputs such as acne severity, suspected melasma, redness/sensitivity, scarring are arguably data about a person's physical condition. The PDPC's draft healthcare guideline (public consultation, July 2026) brings **aesthetic clinics and health spas** into a healthcare-data frame and says **marketing and personalised recommendations need explicit consent**, and recommends **DPIAs for AI-enabled applications** ([HLC / Hogan Lovells summary](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/thailand-pdpc-consults-on-first-healthcarespecific-data-protection-guideline)). Draft, non-binding, and aimed at healthcare providers - but indicative of regulator thinking.
  3. **Photos can reveal other s.26 categories** (ethnic origin, disability, religious dress).
  4. **Regulator posture**: in the Nov 2025 iris-scanning case the PDPC ordered suspension and deletion, and practitioners summarise the stance as "biometric data is high-risk by default" and warn that benefits-for-consent undermine voluntariness ([Chandler MHM, Chambers Data Protection 2026 - Thailand](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/thailand/trends-and-developments)).
  5. **Asymmetric downside**: breaching s.26 carries the top administrative fine (THB 5m, s.84) and unlawful use/disclosure/transfer of s.26 data can be criminal (s.79). A **cosmetics company was fined THB 2.5m in Aug 2025** for inadequate security and failure to notify a breach ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/more-than-a-warning-eight-serious-fines-imposed-in-thai-data-protection-cases/)).
- **Conservative recommendation**: classify face photos and all derived skin-condition attributes as **s.26 sensitive data**; legal basis = **explicit consent**; do not run identification/recognition on the image; do not generate or store face templates; run a DPIA before launch.

### 2.2 Explicit consent - form, content, withdrawal [LAW: s.19, s.23, s.26]

- Consent must be obtained **before or at the time** of collection; requested **explicitly in writing or by electronic means**; **clearly distinguishable from other matters**, in easily accessible, clear and plain language, not deceptive; the controller must inform the **purpose**; consent must be **freely given** and **must not be made a condition of a contract/service for processing that is not necessary for that contract** (s.19).
- **Withdrawal at any time, as easy as giving consent**; the controller must tell the data subject the consequences of withdrawal; a non-compliant consent request is **not binding** (s.19). After withdrawal, erase/anonymise where no other basis exists (s.33(2), s.37(3)).
- **Privacy notice contents (s.23)**: purposes and legal basis; whether provision is required and consequences of not providing; **data collected and retention period** (or expected period by standard); **categories of recipients**; controller / representative / **DPO contact**; data-subject rights.
- PDPC's Sept 2022 consent and notice guidelines (non-binding) add: a **clear affirmative act**, notice may be layered/hyperlinked, inform about cross-border transfers, use age-appropriate language and **age/guardian verification measures** for minors, keep records ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-issues-guidelines-on-pdpa-consent-and-notification-requirements/17/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=TH)).
- **Recommended LINE flow** [INTERPRETATION]: before the first photo, show a dedicated consent card (Flex message or LIFF page) - not the general T&Cs - stating: (1) what is collected (face photo; derived skin attributes; LINE userId); (2) purpose (cosmetic skin-characteristic description and product recommendation; not medical diagnosis; not identification); (3) who processes it (Srichand as controller; named categories: AI model provider, cloud host, LINE) and **that processing may occur outside Thailand in countries that may not have adequate data protection standards**; (4) retention; (5) how to withdraw / delete (a chat keyword and a menu button); (6) age declaration; (7) link to full privacy notice and DPO contact. Require an **unticked-by-default explicit action** ("ยินยอม" button) and log userId, timestamp, consent-text version. Offer a **no-photo path** (questionnaire) so the service is not conditioned on sensitive-data consent. Separate, optional consents for (a) keeping a skin profile over time, (b) marketing/broadcast, (c) use of images for model improvement (default off). Do not reward consent with coupons/points (voluntariness risk per the iris case commentary).

### 2.3 Purpose limitation, minimisation, retention, security

- **Purpose limitation (s.21)**: use only for notified purposes; a new purpose needs new notice and consent. **Minimisation (s.22)**: collection limited to what is necessary for the lawful purpose. **Accuracy (s.35)**.
- **Retention**: PDPA sets **no fixed period**; the period must be stated in the notice (s.23(3)) and the controller must have a system to **erase/destroy when the period ends, data is no longer necessary, the data subject requests it, or consent is withdrawn** (s.37(3)). [INTERPRETATION - recommended default] delete the raw photo immediately after the analysis response is generated (or within a short fixed window such as 24 hours for error handling); retain only non-image derived attributes if the user opted in to a skin profile; contractually require the AI vendor to operate **zero-retention / no-training** on images; purge chat-log image copies on the same schedule (note LINE's own platform retention is outside Srichand's control - disclose it).
- **Security (s.37(1))**: appropriate measures meeting PDPC minimum standards; the 2022 Security Measures Notification (in force 20 June 2022) requires organisational, technical and physical measures including access control, identity proofing/authentication, least-privilege authorisation, and periodic review ([Kennedys summary](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/guidelines-on-key-compliance-requirements-for-the-personal-data-protection-act-in-thailand/)). Controller must also prevent recipients from unlawful use (s.37(2)). The Aug 2025 fines repeatedly cite weak access controls, no risk assessment, no DPA with processor, late/no breach report, and no DPO ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/more-than-a-warning-eight-serious-fines-imposed-in-thai-data-protection-cases/); [Chandler MHM](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/thailand/trends-and-developments)).
- **Records of processing (s.39)** must be kept; the small-organisation exemption in s.39 (and the parallel one for processors in s.40) **does not apply** where the processing involves s.26 data, is non-occasional, or is likely to risk data subjects' rights [LAW].

### 2.4 Data subject rights and timelines [LAW]

- Rights: withdraw consent (s.19); **access/copy and disclosure of source (s.30)**; portability (s.31); **object**, including to direct marketing at any time (s.32); **erasure/destruction/anonymisation (s.33)**; restriction (s.34); rectification (ss.35-36); complain to the expert committee (s.73).
- **Timeline**: access requests must be fulfilled **without delay and within 30 days of receipt** (s.30 para 4; failure: fine up to THB 1m under s.82). The Act text we read does not state a number of days for the other rights; responding within 30 days across all rights is common practice [INTERPRETATION - confirm whether a PDPC sub-regulation now fixes this].
- Refusals must be recorded with reasons in the s.39 record.
- [INTERPRETATION] The bot should recognise rights requests in natural language ("ลบรูปฉัน", "ขอข้อมูลของฉัน", "ยกเลิกความยินยอม"), confirm identity via the LINE userId session, execute deletion automatically where possible, and route the rest to the DPO queue with a 30-day SLA.

### 2.5 Breach notification [LAW: s.37(4)]

Notify the PDPC Office **without delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours** of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in risk to rights and freedoms; if **high risk**, also notify data subjects with remedial measures without delay. Detailed criteria are in the PDPC breach notification of 15 Dec 2022 ([Nishimura & Asahi](https://www.nishimura.com/en/knowledge/publications/personal-data-protection-laws-update)). Breach of s.37: fine up to THB 3m (s.83). **Processors must notify the controller** of breaches (s.40(2)) - the AI vendor contract needs a tight notification clause (PDPC-prescribed transfer clauses contemplate 72-hour party-to-party notice per [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=TH)).

### 2.6 Cross-border transfer (ss.28-29) - key issue for AI model APIs

- **[LAW] s.28**: personal data may be sent abroad only if the destination has **adequate protection standards** under PDPC rules, **except** (among others): (2) **consent, provided the data subject has been informed of the inadequate protection standards** of the destination; (3) necessity for a contract with the data subject or pre-contract steps at their request; (4) contract between controller and another party in the data subject's interest; (1) legal compliance; (5) vital interests; (6) substantial public interest. **s.29**: intra-group transfers under PDPC-certified **BCRs** are exempt; otherwise, absent an adequacy decision, transfer is allowed if the controller/processor provides **appropriate safeguards** enabling enforcement of data-subject rights and effective remedies per PDPC rules.
- **[LAW - via law-firm summaries] PDPC notifications of 25 Dec 2023, effective 24 March 2024**: (i) adequacy criteria (aligned legal measures + an enforcement authority); PDPC may decide case-by-case or issue a list; (ii) appropriate safeguards = **contract clauses** (PDPC-prescribed content, **ASEAN Model Contractual Clauses**, or **EU GDPR SCCs**, with only limited amendments), **certification**, or binding instruments between state agencies; BCR requirements ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-unveils-regulations-for-cross-border-personal-data-transfer/); [Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/en/insights/blogs/digilinks/2024/january/thailand---new-rules-for-transborder-dataflow); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=TH)). The notifications **exclude** from "transfer" mere **intermediary transit** and **storage where no third party can access** the data (e.g. cloud transit) (Linklaters; DLA Piper). BCR certification rules followed on 29 Sep 2025, with first approvals 30 Sep 2025 ([Chandler MHM](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/thailand/trends-and-developments); see also [Baker McKenzie, Apr 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/04/thailand-pdpc-approved-bcrs-for-cross-border-transfers)).
- No published PDPC adequacy "allowlist" was found in the sources reviewed - assume none and rely on safeguards/derogations (verify).
- **Implications** [INTERPRETATION]: sending a customer's face photo to a model API hosted outside Thailand is a transfer to a foreign **processor**. The transit/storage exclusion is unlikely to help because the vendor's systems actively process the image. Compliance path: (a) **Thai-style SCCs / ASEAN MCCs / EU SCCs + DPA** with the vendor (and check its sub-processors and regions), **and** (b) belt-and-braces **explicit consent with the s.28(2) "inadequate standards" warning** in the consent card. Because the data is treated as s.26 data, a transfer breach attracts the **THB 5m** tier (s.84) and potential **criminal liability** (s.79). Prefer an in-region (Thailand/Singapore) endpoint or on-device/edge pre-processing (crop to skin regions, strip EXIF/location) to reduce exposure; SCCs are still needed for any non-Thai region.

### 2.7 Data processor obligations (the AI vendor, cloud host, chatbot integrator) [LAW: s.40]

Processors must: act **only on the controller's instructions**; maintain appropriate **security** and **notify the controller of breaches**; keep **processing records**. A processor that departs from instructions is **deemed a controller**. The controller **must have an agreement** governing the processor's activities (s.40 para 3). Processor fines: up to THB 1m / 3m / 5m (ss.85-87); in Aug 2025 a processor was fined THB 3m, more than its controller ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/more-than-a-warning-eight-serious-fines-imposed-in-thai-data-protection-cases/)). [INTERPRETATION] DPA must cover: purpose lock, no training on customer images, retention/deletion, sub-processor approval, locations, audit, breach notice timing, assistance with data-subject requests.

### 2.8 DPO requirement [LAW: s.41 + PDPC notification]

A DPO is mandatory where (2) activities require **regular monitoring of personal data/systems on a large scale** as prescribed by the PDPC, or (3) the **core activity is processing s.26 data**. The PDPC DPO notification (effective 13 Dec 2023) treats as large-scale, among others, processing of **100,000+ data subjects**, and systematic tracking/profiling as regular monitoring ([Baker McKenzie via Global Compliance News](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2023/10/19/https-insightplus-bakermckenzie-com-bm-investigations-compliance-ethics-thailand-pdpa-update-sub-regulation-regarding-the-designation-of-data-protection-officers-published-in-the-government-gazette_/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=TH)). Failure: fine up to THB 1m (s.82), and recent cases treat a missing DPO as aggravating ([Chandler MHM](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/thailand/trends-and-developments)). [INTERPRETATION] A national brand's LINE OA with profiling-based recommendations very likely crosses the threshold - confirm a DPO is appointed, registered with the PDPC, and named in the consent card.

### 2.9 Minors (SASI targets teens) [LAW: s.20]

- A **minor** = under **20** and not sui juris by marriage (Civil and Commercial Code). Rules: **under 10** - consent must come from the **parent/guardian**, always; **10 to under 20** - if giving the consent is **not** an act the minor may do alone under CCC ss.22-24 (acts purely acquiring a right/escaping a duty; strictly personal acts; acts suitable to the minor's station in life and needed for reasonable needs), **the parent/guardian's consent is also required**. The same applies to withdrawal, notices, rights requests and complaints (s.20 last para).
- PDPC consent guideline: age-appropriate language, **appropriate age-verification and guardian-identification measures**, and record-keeping ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-issues-guidelines-on-pdpa-consent-and-notification-requirements/17/); [Securiti summary](https://securiti.ai/blog/consent-requirements-under-thailands-data-protection-framework/)); controllers must exercise heightened care with minors' data ([Chandler MHM](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/thailand/trends-and-developments)).
- **Conservative position** [INTERPRETATION]: explicit consent to AI analysis of a teen's face photo (sensitive data, possible overseas transfer) is unlikely to fall within CCC ss.22-24, so **users aged 10-19 need parental consent as well as their own; under-10s must not use the photo feature**. Practical design: age gate at onboarding; for under-20s, default to the **no-photo questionnaire path** with no retention, or implement a verifiable parental-consent step; no profiling-based marketing to minors; teen-safe product set (no peels/retinoid-type actives without caution, no whitening pressure). Whether buying low-value cosmetics by a teen is a valid contract under CCC s.24 is a separate contract-law question (likely yes for ordinary purchases - confirm).

| Administrative | s.83 (purpose/minimisation ss.21-22, legal basis s.24, use/disclosure s.27, transfer s.28, security & breach s.37, deceptive consent) | THB 3,000,000 |
| Administrative | s.84 (any of the above involving **s.26 sensitive data**; s.26 itself) | THB 5,000,000 |
| Administrative (processor) | ss.85-87 | THB 1m / 3m / 5m |
| Civil | s.77 compensation regardless of intent or negligence (limited defences); s.78 court may add **punitive damages up to 2x actual damages**; limitation 3 years from knowledge / 10 years from act | - |
| Criminal | s.79: unlawful use/disclosure (s.27) or transfer (s.28) of **s.26 data** likely to cause damage/humiliation: up to **6 months and/or THB 500,000**; if for unlawful benefit: up to **1 year and/or THB 1,000,000** (compoundable) | - |
| Criminal | s.81: **directors/managers** liable where the company's offence results from their order, act or omission | same as offence |

Enforcement trend: eight fines totalling about THB 21.5m announced 1 Aug 2025, with a proactive "Eagle Eye" monitoring unit and public naming of sectors ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/more-than-a-warning-eight-serious-fines-imposed-in-thai-data-protection-cases/); [Chandler MHM](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/thailand/trends-and-developments)). The Emergency Decree on Technology Crimes (No. 2) B.E. 2568 adds criminal penalties for personal-data misuse linked to tech crime (Chandler MHM).

### 2.11 AI, automated decision-making, profiling; draft AI law

- **PDPA**: the Act contains **no GDPR-Art.22-style right** regarding automated decisions/profiling (checked against the full text; the only "automated" references are in s.31 portability). Profiling matters indirectly: it is a DPO trigger (2.8) and direct-marketing profiling can be objected to at any time (s.32(2)).
- **PDPC guidance**: as of March 2026 there was **no binding PDPC guidance or law on AI/automated decision-making**; regulator expectations are risk-based: **DPIA**, transparency about AI use, data governance, human oversight ([Chandler MHM](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/thailand/trends-and-developments)). A PDPA amendment bill was in consultation in 2025 (revisiting sensitive-data definitions, controller/processor roles, consent voluntariness) - not enacted as of that source.
- **ETDA / MDES soft law** (non-binding): AI Governance Guideline for Executives (2023) ([ETDA PDF](https://www.etda.or.th/getattachment/Our-Service/AIGC/Research-and-Recommendation/Thailand%E2%80%99s-AI-Governance-Guideline-for-Executive_2023.pdf.aspx?lang=th-TH)); **Generative AI Governance Guideline for Organisations, 30 Oct 2024** ([DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/thailand-mdes-and-etda-issue-guidelines-genai); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/24106-issued-de-and-etda-generative-ai-governance-guideline-for-organisations-including-guidelines-for-the-ethical-application-of-generative-ai-for-organisations)).
- **Draft AI Act (status Sept 2026: not law)**: ETDA released a new draft in early July 2026 (sources give 2 and 9 July) for public consultation closing **14 Aug 2026**. Risk-based: **prohibited AI** (e.g. manipulative, unfairly discriminatory), **high-risk AI** (sectors to be designated), AI requiring registration/licensing, and **transparency obligations for chatbots, generative AI and deepfakes**; applies to developers, providers, **deployers** and platforms incl. offshore; strict and joint liability; powers to suspend/block systems; administrative fines reported up to **THB 5m**; deployer duties for high-risk AI include risk management and log retention (6 months proposed). Next steps: ETDA review, then Cabinet approval in principle, then Parliament ([Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/thailand-draft-ai-act--seven-key-implications); [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/thailand-moves-towards-comprehensive-ai-regulation-with-new-draft-ai-act); [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/thailands-proposed-ai-law-moving-towards-a-risk-based-regulatory-framework/)). [INTERPRETATION] Design now for the likely end-state: always disclose that the user is talking to an AI, label AI-generated images, keep decision logs, provide a human hand-off.

## 3. Other relevant rules (brief)

### 3.1 Consumer Protection Act B.E. 2522 (as amended) - advertising
s.22 mirrors the Cosmetics Act: no unfair statements - false or exaggerated, misleading on essential elements, etc. Under **s.21**, where a specific law (here the Cosmetics Act) governs a matter, that law applies and the CPA applies only so far as not repetitive or contrary - but the OCPB committees can step in if the sector regulator does not act ([CPA English text](https://www.aseanconsumer.org/file/post_image/The%20Consumer%20Protection%20Act%201979.pdf)). Penalties after the 2019 amendment: intentionally misleading advertising/labels - up to **6 months and/or THB 100,000**, doubled for repeat offences (s.47); breach of s.22(3)-(5) or advertising rules ss.23-26 - up to 3 months and/or THB 60,000 (s.48); disobeying Advertising Committee orders - up to 6 months and/or THB 100,000 (s.49) ([CPA (No.4) B.E. 2562 English text](https://aseanconsumer.org/file/pdf_file/consumer%20protection%20act%20no.4.pdf)). Relevance: promotions, price claims, "free gift" statements and testimonials generated by the AI.

### 3.2 Direct Sales and Direct Marketing Act B.E. 2545 (as amended 2017)
"Direct marketing" (ตลาดแบบตรง) = marketing goods/services by communicating information directly to consumers at a distance expecting a response purchase; operators must **register with the OCPB before operating** and lodge a security deposit (THB 25,000 for a legal entity with revenue under THB 25m; up to THB 200,000 above) ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/legal/e-commerce-and-direct-marketing); [LawPlus](https://www.lawplusltd.com/2018/12/new-rules-direct-sales-direct-marketing/)). Two Ministerial Regulations effective 20 Nov 2018 exempt only individuals with online revenue not exceeding THB 1.8m/year, SMEs registered under the SME promotion law, community/social enterprises and cooperatives (LawPlus). Selling through social chat such as LINE is generally treated as direct marketing; industry sources cite penalties for operating unregistered of up to 1 year and/or THB 100,000 plus THB 10,000/day ([MakeWebEasy](https://www.makewebeasy.com/th/blog/direct-marketing-registration/) - secondary; section numbers not verified). The Act also gives consumers a cooling-off/cancellation right and requires sales documents (details to confirm). Action: confirm Srichand (or the selling entity behind the LINE OA) holds a **direct marketing registration** covering chat commerce.

### 3.3 E-commerce commercial registration (DBD) and online price display
Under the Commercial Registration Act B.E. 2499 regime, online sellers had to register e-commerce with the DBD; a Ministry of Commerce notification effective **5 June 2024** exempts **juristic persons already registered with the DBD** (limited companies, public companies, registered partnerships, etc.) from a separate e-commerce commercial registration, while natural persons still must register; the direct-marketing registration remains separately required ([FRANK Legal & Tax via Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/thailand-updates-e-commerce-regulations-what-you-need-to-know/)). The "DBD Registered" trust mark is optional but helpful ([Thailand.go.th](https://www.thailand.go.th/issue-focus-detail/006_092)). **Price display**: notifications of the Central Committee on Prices of Goods and Services under the Price of Goods and Services Act B.E. 2542 require online sellers to **show price, type, size/weight and details clearly and openly, plus any extra charges** - "inbox for price" is not allowed; fine up to **THB 10,000** ([Bangkokbiznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/business/859860)). The notification number is re-issued periodically (sources cite different numbers) - verify current one. AI implication: always quote full price, size, shipping cost and promotion conditions in the chat before order confirmation.

### 3.4 LINE Official Account terms
LINE's OA Terms of Use (Thailand) make the account owner responsible for **all applicable laws** (Arts. 4, 10), require **compliance with personal-data law including obtaining users' prior consent** for data the business collects directly (Art. 8), prohibit intentionally false information and content that is a nuisance to users (Art. 17), and allow LINE to delete content or suspend/terminate without notice (Arts. 7, 18) ([LINE OA Terms of Use TH](https://terms2.line.me/official_account_terms_th)). The OA Guidelines (TH) bar or restrict, among others, medical products unapproved in Thailand, sexual-enhancement products and **cosmetics referencing sexual function**, content "guaranteeing... recovery from sickness", content inappropriate for minors, and marketing exploiting users' suffering or fear ([LINE OA Guideline TH](https://terms2.line.me/official_account_guideline_th?lang=en)). Friends can block the OA at any time; broadcasts reach only friends. [INTERPRETATION] Adding the OA as a friend is **not** PDPA consent to profiling-based marketing; keep a separate marketing opt-in/opt-out, honour s.32 objections, and cap broadcast frequency (Cosmetics Act s.42 "annoyance" and LINE Art. 17).

### 3.5 Computer Crime Act B.E. 2550 (amended 2560)
s.14(1): dishonestly or deceptively entering **distorted or false computer data** likely to cause damage to the public - up to **5 years and/or THB 100,000** (lower, compoundable tier where the victim is an individual); the 2017 amendment re-focused it on fraud/forgery rather than defamation ([iLaw on s.14(1)](https://www.ilaw.or.th/articles/8839); [iLaw on elements of the offence](https://www.ilaw.or.th/articles/4748)). Thai authorities have used it alongside FDA laws against online sellers making false product claims. For a legitimate brand the realistic exposure is a knowingly false efficacy or "FDA-certified" statement pushed at scale; a hallucinating bot is an operational risk here, so ground every product fact in the approved knowledge base.

## 4. Hard rules for the AI agent (distilled)

1. Never say a product **treats, cures, heals, prevents or reduces** acne, melasma, freckles, dark spots, rash, inflammation, infection, hair loss or any named condition (Cosmetics Act s.41(3); FDA manual).
2. Never claim **physiological mechanisms or structural change**: collagen/elastin stimulation, melanin/tyrosinase inhibition, cell repair/renewal, circulation, detoxification, lifting/face-slimming, fat loss, pore shrinking, penetration below the epidermis (s.41; Ministerial Regulation 2024).
3. Use **appearance language + mandatory qualifiers**: "แลดู...", "ช่วยให้ผิวดู...", "ผลลัพธ์ขึ้นอยู่กับสภาพผิวของแต่ละบุคคล", whitening = cannot change natural skin colour, anti-wrinkle = effect only while using.
4. No **superlatives, guarantees, time-bound promises, "safe/no side effects/non-irritating" absolutes, "FDA-approved"**, no comparison with injections/laser/Botox, no naming competitors.
5. Cite **tests, awards, sales ranks** only from the approved evidence file, with sample size, period, source.
6. No **doctor persona or medical endorsement**; no hospital/clinic words or medical symbols; no before/after images unless legal has cleared a lab-confirmed set.
7. **Never diagnose.** Describe visible characteristics only; add the non-diagnosis disclaimer; refer to a dermatologist on red flags.
8. SPF/PA: quote only the tested label value; add correct-use advice; never "100%/all-day/no re-application".
9. **No photo before explicit consent**; offer a no-photo path; treat photo and skin attributes as sensitive data; minimal retention; honour delete/withdraw commands instantly; age gate (under 20 needs parental consent; under 10 blocked).
10. Always disclose **AI identity**, quote **full prices and conditions**, and hand off to a human on complaints, adverse reactions, medical questions, minors' issues or data-rights requests.
